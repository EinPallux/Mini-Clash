## [Unreleased] — v0.6.0 "The Living Bridge" (in progress)

### Added
- **The map runs a timetable (GAME_DESIGN §9).** Six windows — three fixed anchors at 2:00, 6:00 and 10:30, three seeded pools at 4:00, 8:30 and 12:30 — are rolled once at match start from the match seed. A horn and a banner call each one 8 s ahead, a ticker chip counts the live window down, and the minimap glows where it is about to happen. The schedule is reproducible in a replay and plannable by bots because it comes off the seed, not off wall-clock chance. Missing the announce means missing the window: nothing re-announces, and the ticker only names the *next* event inside 30 s — which is what makes Orb Sense's extra 10 s a card rather than a copy of the default HUD.
- **Flank Isles.** Two 10×6 platforms haul themselves out of the void 14.5 u north and south of mid, each carrying a health orb, each joined to the deck by a light-bridge you can actually walk. They open a genuine flank route for 60 s and then fall, putting anyone still standing on one back on the deck first. The nav grid had to grow for this — the isles float in space that has to exist before it can be opened, so the world is now 38 u deep while the playable deck stays 18.
- **Coin Rain.** A marked zone showers thirty coins over 20 s, 2–6 gold each, paid on touch to whoever gets there first. Coins outlive the shower on their own 8 s timers, so the scramble does not end when the rain does.
- **Storm Front.** A full-width wall of lightning crosses the bridge end to end over 25 s, burning 2.5% max HP a second and slowing 15%. Its position is a pure function of elapsed time rather than a wandering entity, so it is exact at any tick rate — and it is *not* outrun: the wall moves at 5.3 u/s against a champion's 3.6, so running with it keeps you inside for 2.4 s while walking into the oncoming edge costs 0.45 s. Veteran and Elite bots already know that.
- **The Clash Golem, and the Elder.** A neutral golem wakes on the altar at 6:00 and slams anyone who comes close. It does not die to its first killer — **it changes sides**: whoever lands the killing blow takes it, and it marches that team's lane as a siege engine, shrugging off 40% of tower fire and amping the Minis travelling with it. The Elder at 10:30 is 60% bigger and shields allied champions on top. It is built from vendored CC0 Kenney stone on a hand-keyed rig — a stacked construct with a wind-up-and-smash slam, a heavy walk cycle driven by distance covered, and an amber core in its chest that re-tints to the captor's colour the instant it converts. That colour is the read: amber means the objective is still on the table.
- **Bridge Collapse.** From Overtime the outer 3 u of both long edges tear away every 60 s, narrowing the deck 18 → 12 → 8 u. Real floor tiles tumble into the void on a staggered delay so the strip tears rather than dropping as a slab, the rails and brush go with them, units standing on the doomed ground are swept inward first, and the minimap strip visibly shrinks inside its frame.
- **Bots read the timetable.** Veterans and Elites contest the golem — and an Elite reads its health bar, diving a nearly-dead one from well outside its usual engage range because the killing blow is the whole objective. They detour for Coin Rain gold when it is genuinely on the way, cross the Storm Front the fast way, and never path onto a strip the Collapse has taken. Recruits ignore all of it, which is exactly how a beginner plays.
- **A sim tick budget test.** The busiest state the game can produce — Overtime, all-Ram waves, a collapsing deck — is now measured per tick against the frame budget, because v0.6 regressed exactly there once (see Fixed).

### Balance
- **A converted Clash Golem sieges for 90 s and then crumbles.** It had no lifetime: it marched until something killed it, which for the 10:30 Elder usually meant "until the match ended". Across 40 harness matches (78 golem takes) the team that landed the killing blow won **69%** — over the phase's 65% acceptance rail, and the Elder alone ran 72%. Some of that is correlation (the team winning the fight at the altar takes the golem), but a permanent extra unit is not what §9 describes: the golem is a *siege engine*, and a siege is a window. Take it, turn it into towers, or get nothing.

### Changed
- **Live event timers ride the frame header, not the match blob.** They tick every frame, so leaving them in the JSON marked the whole of match state — event log included — dirty twenty times a second. Encoded as ~8 bytes of header instead, the wire came down to **9.8 KB/s** mid-game and **8.3 KB/s** through the golem window, against a 12 KB/s budget. The budget test now measures both windows and asserts the golem is actually on the field in the second one, so a quiet sample cannot pass for free.
- **Event Insurance pays out only inside its window.** The card said "dying within 10 s of a Living Bridge event start" and was implemented as a flat respawn cut, which is a much stronger card than the one the catalog describes.

### Fixed
- **A\* was 64% of a bot match.** Minis re-path when their goal drifts, and a goal that is *another unit* drifts every tick — so a 120-strong mid-lane brawl ran roughly a hundred full searches per tick. Minis now steer straight at a goal that is close and unobstructed and only plan a route when they actually need one; a full bot match went from 151 s to 51 s, and a busy Overtime tick measures **0.4 ms**.
- **A failed path was retried every tick.** An unreachable goal costs A* a full exhaustion of the reachable set before it can say "no", so a handful of stuck units could eat the frame — measured at 12 searches a tick and 7.6 s per 300 ticks. Failures now back off half a second; the same 300 ticks take 619 ms.
- **Opening the isles could leak permanently blocked nav cells.** `openRegion` recorded the prior value of cells carrying a live wall stamp; by the time the region was restored, the stamp's owner had already unstamped — it saw an opened cell and did nothing — so the restore resurrected an obstacle nobody owned. One match leaked 259 cells and froze 155 of 171 Minis behind them. Only static geometry opens now.
- **A converted golem marched backwards.** It started at lane step 0, which for the eastern team is its own gate. It now picks up the lane at the waypoint nearest wherever it converted.
- **The neutral golem could not be attacked by half the map.** It carries a team tag while neutral because the field is not nullable, and attack validation was reading that tag — so team 0 could not hit the objective it was supposed to be racing for. Attackability is decided by ownership now.
- **The spawn barrier was sized off the nav grid**, so once the world grew for the isles it hung out over open sky. It spans the deck.
- **The minimap has been invisible since v0.2.** `.match-root canvas { position: absolute; inset: 0 }` is a *descendant* selector, and the lane strip's canvas lives inside the HUD, inside `.match-root` — so it was pinned across the whole screen and collapsed its own container to nothing. Found while asserting the v0.6 acceptance line that every event stays readable with the sound off, which leans on the minimap glow. Scoped to the direct child.

### Docs
- GAME_DESIGN §9.1 records the rules the timetable actually runs on: the 30 s reveal window, that a missed announce stays missed, that Overtime ends the timetable and retires any event still holding terrain, where the isles sit and why, and the Storm Front's crossing maths.
- ASSET_CATALOG §3: the Clash Golem ships as a kitbash of vendored CC0 Kenney stone rather than the planned Quaternius mesh, which needs a network fetch this build environment cannot make. Written down as what shipped and why, not as a gap.

## [Unreleased] — v0.5.0 "Power Surge" (in progress)

### Added
- **The draft (AUGMENTS.md §1, UI_UX §9):** hitting level **3, 6 or 9** deals you three cards, and **the match does not stop**. The dock slides up bottom-centre above the ability cluster and the fight carries on around it — picking mid-teamfight is a real decision under fire, which is the whole point. `1/2/3` or a click takes a card, `R` spends the match's single reroll on the whole set, and a 45 s conic ring counts down (it turns hot and pulses under 10 s). Nothing in the overlay calls `preventDefault`, so movement and ability keys reach the game exactly as they do with it closed — the headless smoke asserts it, walking 4.5 u and putting Q on cooldown with the dock open. At 0 the utility scorer picks for you and the confirmation slab says **COACH CHOSE** instead of **ACQUIRED**; the card then settles into a compact strip beside your abilities. Cards carry a rarity frame (silver, gold, and a prismatic that shimmers through a slow rainbow sweep), the owning champion's name on signatures, a category glyph, the effect line and its on-screen tell. Prismatics in the hand get their own chase-card sting.
- **The catalog is real, not declared (docs/AUGMENTS.md):** all **78** cards — 48 generic + 3 signatures × 10 champions — do something on the field. The generic vocabulary carries most of it: stat patches, conditional damage, basic-attack riders, duo/economy modifiers, ultimate potency, and named `param` knobs that let a signature reshape its own champion's kit without the engine learning which champion is which. Three **cast modifiers** land as engine work: *Splitter* fires the Q as a V of two weaker casts, *Mirror Strike* sends a second one at the nearest **other** enemy, and *Echo Cast* repeats the W from the same spot a beat later — the delayed halves ride the deterministic world scheduler and re-read the caster on arrival, so dying or swapping out cancels the encore. The bespoke cards are hooked into the systems they belong to: Elemental Ascension rolls its element off the match seed at pickup and then burns, chills or refunds Energy on every ability hit; Deathblossom detonates the body; Thornmail Soul answers basics; Second Wind regrows you once per life; Guardian Constellation banks a star that eats one whole ability; **Undying Contract** refuses your first death of the match and brings the bench up on the spot at 40%; Kinetic Battery fills from distance run and is spent by the hit that reads it; Double Feature leaves the outgoing half on stage as a ghost that keeps swinging. Every signature does what its card says, down to Rook's rampart eating projectiles, Rattle's daggers coming back through what they missed, Wisp's popped decoy detonating, Piper's stampede finishing on elephants, and Vex's third lash calling the debt. `silence` joins the CC vocabulary as a real primitive (hands locked, feet free) because Mortis' *Restricted Section* asks for exactly that and a stun would have been a lie.
- **Champions +2 — Piper and Vex (CHAMPIONS.md):** the roster reaches **10**, and their signatures ship with them.
- **Discovery: scouting is the price of knowing (AUGMENTS.md §1, UI_UX §11).** Enemy augments are not handed to you. The sim tracks, per team, which cards it has actually *seen* on the field — owner alive, visible, not tucked in a brush — and rewrites everything else to `?` before the snapshot crosses the wire, so a client physically cannot render what it was never sent. The count is public, the identities are earned. The trio now shows on the Tab scoreboard, the end-of-match summary, kill cards (what killed you is also *how*), and as three small rarity chips over each enemy's in-world nameplate, grey while unknown. Local match history keeps your own trio and each seat's as-you-saw-it list.
- **Bots draft (AUGMENTS.md §2):** offers are scored by kit-tag affinity, team-composition gaps, a comeback nudge when behind, and seeded personality noise — and bots commit early rather than idling out the timer, so a draft never freezes a bot mid-fight.
- **The Training Grounds drafts too.** Levelling to 3/6/9 in the Grounds opens a real draft, and the trainer panel has a **Deal augment draft** button so you can try any card on any kit at any level. Switching champion there clears the loadout, so a signature can never ride along onto a champion it was never written for.

### Changed
- **A max-HP card grants its delta** instead of quietly shaving a slice off your bar mid-fight — the same rule a level-up already used.
- **Draft privacy is enforced on the wire, not just in the snapshot.** `draftOpen`/`draftReroll`/`augmentPicked` now carry a team and the server scopes them like pings, so an enemy can never read your offers or learn a pick before the card shows itself on the field.
- **Augment state lives in its own bag** (`augState`) on the seat. Unlike passive scratch it is **not** exchanged on a Tag Swap, because a `[duo]` card belongs to the seat rather than to whichever half is on stage.
- **The balance harness reports augments.** Every run now prints pick-when-offered and winrate per card against the acceptance rails (generics: ≤65% pick, ≤56% win), with signatures listed separately — a near-auto-pick on the kit they were written for is the design, not a flag.

### Balance
- **Undying Contract** 40% → **30%** revive HP, and the half that steps in is now stuck out there for a full swap cooldown. A refused death that is *also* a free swap is a strict upgrade, and the cost is what makes the card a decision. **Note on how this was arrived at:** the change was first made because the 60-match report showed it taken 74% of the times it was offered against a 65% rail. Re-running afterwards, the pick rate went **up**, to 79% — because `scoreOffer` never reads an augment's parameters at all. It scored by rarity, category and tags, so nothing about hpFrac or a swap cooldown was visible to the thing being measured. The nerf stands on its own design argument; the harness evidence for it did not exist.
- **Bot draft scoring: the rarity prior was drowning the kit affinity.** It ran 1 / 2 / 3 for silver / gold / prismatic, against tag matches worth 0.4–1.0 each — so no amount of fit could beat a tier, bots took the shiniest card in the hand, and the pick-rate rail was measuring *this weight* rather than the augment. Now 1 / 1.25 / 1.6, small enough that two good tag matches outrank a rarity step. This is a bot-competence fix as much as a measurement one: AUGMENTS §2 promises elites respect combo lines, and they were not. Re-run on the identical seed, Undying Contract went **79% → 39%** pick-when-offered and every generic landed under the 65% rail.
- **Piper 35% → 43% and Wisp 38% → 44%**, both into the 40–60 band, as a knock-on of bots drafting to their kits (Piper also took the durability nudge above). That leaves **Fathom 64%** and **Grukk 61%** as the only champions outside the band at n≈60 — a new signal from this run, marginal at that sample, and left for the nightly sweep rather than chased with another 40 matches.
- **Piper** 640 → **690** base HP (73.33 → 75 per level), 22 → **24** base armor (2.44 → 2.6 per level). She landed at **35% over ~100 games** with a 6.4/8.8 K/D — a support dying too often to do her job. Sylva, the other support, sits at 49% with 705 HP and 24 armor; this closes most of that gap without touching the kit.

### Docs
- AUGMENTS §3.3 #15 (Elemental Ascension) drops the "choose at pickup" wording: the shipped card rolls its element off the match seed, which is what the data has always said and what determinism requires.
- AUGMENTS #29, CHAMPIONS Piper stat row, GAME_DESIGN §10.3 (Silence) and UI_UX §9/§11 all re-synced to what shipped.

## [Unreleased] — v0.4.0 "Tag Team" (in progress)

### Added
- **Bots swap, tier-appropriately (GAME_DESIGN §7.2):** the bench recovers while it sits, so the swap is fundamentally a resource play — and the brains now play it that way. **Recruits never swap**, because playing one half is exactly what a beginner does. **Veterans** cycle the bench for Energy and cooldowns (spend a spent half, bring in a loaded one) and burn the swap-in haste as a free disengage when they're running. **Elites** add range counter-pivots (bring in whichever half fits the current engagement distance) and entrance weaving (swap *into* Rook's Shieldwall, Mortis's nova, Grukk's toll aura, Wisp's chill-and-untargetable Cold Spot when a fight is already on them) — and refuse to trade away an ultimate that is up mid-fight, which suppresses some of their swaps. (An earlier draft of this entry claimed that made elites swap outright *less often* than veterans — one measurement said so, and v0.5's augments later reversed it. Swap frequency is context noise; the A/B below is the claim that holds.) Proven, not asserted: `scripts/swap-ab.mjs` runs both teams on identical duo deals with one side pinned to a single half (`rig.noSwapTeam`), alternating which side may swap so side bias cancels — the swapping side won **92.5% over 40 elite matches**, against a 55% acceptance bar, with the pinned side verified at exactly 0 swaps. Five sim tests lock the tier ladder, the rig's honesty, and the shared pool surviving a full swapping match.
- **Duos everywhere on the stats surfaces (UI_UX §10–§12):** kill cards now show the **pair** that got the kill and the pair that lost the fight — active half solid, benched half tucked behind it and dimmed. The Tab scoreboard carries duo chips for both teams (concealed enemies keep a two-slot `?` so the shape still reads), the match summary spells the pairing out under each name ("Mortis + Fathom"), the podium labels the MVP's duo, and the stored match history keeps both halves per seat (the history *viewer* arrives with the hub in v0.7). **Training got a duo-config panel:** pick any champion as your bench — or Solo — and practise Space against that exact pairing without leaving the Grounds; the shared HP pool re-forms on the spot (Rook + Wisp reads 838, the average of 1050 and 625).
- **Champions +2 — Boltz and Wisp (CHAMPIONS.md §4):** the roster hits **8**, which also closes the duo-dealing gap (four duos need eight distinct champions — the deal no longer has to exhaust the roster). **Boltz, Salvage Unit 7** — a gunner/specialist whose *Capacitor* primes after three quiet seconds so the next shot lands charged and arcs to a second target (the visor glows while it's held); *Arc Zapper* is an instant hitscan tesla beam that bites harder into shielded targets and refunds Energy off champions; *Bubble Dome* projects a hex shell that **pops enemy projectiles at the surface** while allies inside fire faster from within it; *Orbital Droppod* calls a pod down after a telegraphed 1.2 s — impact damage and a knock-up, then the pod **stays four seconds as a real bunker** (nav-stamped: it blocks movement and projectiles both) before igniting and launching away. His *EVA Hop* entrance is a jetpack skip on arrival. **Wisp, the Unfinished Goodbye** — a caster/trickster whose *Ectoplasm* means she **never collides with anything**, chilling whatever she drifts through; *Boo!* is a spook-bolt that hits Chilled targets 25% harder and startles Minis; *Sheet Slip* blinks her out and leaves the sheet behind as a decoy that taunts nearby Minis and takes **exactly two hits** regardless of size, while she goes invisible until she casts or attacks; *Haunting Hour* curses the ground for four seconds — continuous Chill and damage, Minis inside fight for **nobody**, and at the midnight gong everyone still standing there is **Feared** away from the center. Her *Cold Spot* entrance chills in a nova and leaves her untargetable through the swap morph — the only entrance that touches the swap itself.
  New generic sim capabilities backing them, all data-driven opcodes with zod schemas: hitscan `beam` corridors (with a shield-conditional multiplier and an Energy refund hook), deployable `field` zones in two flavours (projectile-popping dome, nav-stamping bunker with a telegraphed delayed drop and impact burst), `curse` ground (enemy DoT + refreshed debuff + Mini disable + an expiry effect), and `blink` with an optional decoy and self-buff — plus **champion fear** as a real CC (flee away from a point with controls and swaps locked, interrupting casts, cleared on respawn), projectile `bonusVsBuff`, and `NavGrid.stampDisc` for circular temporary obstacles. Twelve kit tests cover it end-to-end, including a live projectile actually dying on a dome shell, the pod blocking and then releasing the deck, the Capacitor arc jumping to a second target, the decoy's two-hit rule, and the gong's fear.
- **Tag Swap is playable (GAME_DESIGN §7.2, UI_UX §8):** **Space** swaps your duo mid-match. The morph is **client-predicted** — the squash-and-pop starts on the keypress rather than a round trip later, measured at **1 ms input→morph over a 544 ms round trip** (the ROADMAP acceptance asks for ≤50 ms at 80 ms RTT). The presentation reads as one motion: a white-and-gold puff at the feet, the model squashing to nothing and popping back out with the rig exchanged exactly at the midpoint so the switch is never visible, a ring flash and light pulse, an arrival chime, and the incoming champion's entrance effect. The **duo panel** sits beside the ability cluster: the benched portrait ringed by the 9 s swap radial (label flips to SPACE when ready), dimmed while on cooldown, glinting when the bench brings both a castable ability and the Energy to pay for it, with its own Energy bar alongside — swapping into an empty bar is a real cost. Duo state rides the binary codec: the timers share the cooldown block (they tick together) and the bench identity rides the rare block (it only moves on a swap), keeping the wire at ~9.7 KB/s against the 12 KB/s budget. Space is rebindable in Settings → Controls; the lag smoke now measures swap feel in-page every CI run.
- **Duo dealing in champion select (GAME_DESIGN §7.1):** the Deal now hands every player a **pair**. Your duo lands as two chained cards (ACTIVE ♦ BENCH) with a dice chip on each — the 2 rerolls are spent per-slot, so you can re-draw the half you don't want and keep the one you do. Rerolled champions slide to the shared team bench and swap back into whichever slot you tap (the selected slot is outlined, and the bench label says where it will land). A reroll always changes the card, and a duo never holds the same champion twice — including when the roster is smaller than the deal wants (a 4-duo team needs 8 distinct champions; with 6 the deal exhausts the roster before it repeats, and Boltz and Wisp close that gap later this phase). The timer moves 45 s → 60 s per the design. Ally seats show their pairs live, enemy silhouettes carry a duo marker but stay hidden, and the tip line reads the passive of whichever half you have selected plus a Space-swap reminder. Both drivers deal duos: offline locally, and the lobby **server-side** (`LobbyRoom` deals 8 team-unique champions as 4 pairs, validates per-slot rerolls and bench swaps, and composes a roster where every seat carries `benchId`). The loading veil shows both halves of each ally pair. Covered by the updated lobby round-trip test and both CI browser smokes.
- **Tag Swap sim core (GAME_DESIGN §7.2):** duos live in the simulation — `MatchPlayerConfig` takes a `benchId`, and the champion entity carries a duo bank: the benched champion's kit, cooldowns (which keep ticking), Energy (which keeps refilling — swapping is a resource play), and passive scratch. The `swap` intent exchanges the banks in place: HP (one shared pool, the average of both champions' HP curves — symmetric, so swapping never moves your health bar), position, items, level, gold, buffs and shields all stay put. 9 s swap cooldown, 0.35 s morph (you keep moving and stay targetable, but attacks/casts wait), +20% move speed decaying over 1 s on swap-in, and the arriving champion's entrance effect fires. Blocked while channeling, leaping, stunned or airborne — a basic-attack windup just cancels. Death kills the duo on one timer; respawn restores both Energy bars with swap ready. Solo configs (v0.3 shape) are untouched. Seven edge-case sim tests: shared-pool math across swaps and level-ups, per-champion banks with bench recovery, morph lockout, channel/CC blocks, buff carryover, swap-adjacent projectile flight, death-frame handling, and snapshot duo blocks.

### Changed
- **The balance harness deals duos now — it never did before.** `botmatch.mjs` handed every seat a solo champion with no bench, so every balance number the project has published to date measured pre-Tag-Team play. It now deals four pairs per team the way champion select does, credits a result to **both** halves of a duo (crediting whichever kit happened to be active on the final tick was a coin flip), reports **duo-pairing** winrates alongside champion ones, and counts Tag Swaps per seat.
  It also gained `--free-duos`, and the reason is worth writing down: select-accurate dealing hands each team 8 *team-unique* halves, so at a roster of exactly 8 **both teams field the entire roster and every match is a mirror** — champion winrate is pinned at 50% by construction and measures nothing at all. `--free-duos` draws per seat so teams differ, making champion strength observable again. It is a measurement instrument, not how the game deals; the shipped dealing is judged on pairings.

### Fixed
- **SpaceKit meshes were authored off-origin** (`bboxMin [1.1, 0, 0.6]` — their position in the pack's showcase grid, not the model origin), so Boltz's droppod landed ~3.5 u to the side of its own impact crater and his raygun hung in the air beside his hand. Fixed at the source: the asset pipeline gained an opt-in `recenter` that pulls a scene onto its horizontal footprint with the base at y = 0 and re-measures the bbox, applied to the two SpaceKit entries. Opt-in rather than blanket, so the already-tuned centered models don't shift by a few centimetres.
- **The droppod rendered twice** — once as an FX-timeline prop and once as the zone actor's bunker. The pod body now belongs solely to the zone actor (it has to survive the full 4 s and launch away), and slams the last few metres on arrival instead.
- **The Bubble Dome rendered as a solid white ball.** Additive blending with `DoubleSide` stacks front and back faces, which clips to white over the bright deck; the volume fill is now whisper-thin and a facet wireframe carries the hex-shield read. Same class of bug on Boltz's helmet, where a hot specular highlight sat exactly under the top-down camera.
- **Boltz and Wisp were using the placeholder crossed-swords ability icon** — the other six champions have bespoke glyphs. Six new ones drawn to match the set (bolt, dome, droppod, ghost, sheet, midnight clock).

### Assets
- **The astronaut rig job is closed by dropping it.** `astronautA/B` ship unrigged (0 skins, 0 clips) and the plan of record was a Blender headless weight-transfer — a binary art-tool dependency bolted onto a pipeline that is otherwise pure gltf-transform, for exactly one champion. **Boltz wears the astronaut instead of being it:** `chars/character-male-c` (Kenney-Skinned, full 17-clip vocabulary, so he animates like every other Kenney champion) + visor palette + a procedural bubble-helmet dome that doubles as his Capacitor telegraph, with the SpaceKit raygun at the hand socket. Four new pipeline entries (`chars/character-male-c`, `graveyard/ghost`, `space/weapon-gun`, `space/rocket-pod`); the pack was already CC0-cleared and credited. Wisp needed nothing special — `character-ghost` ships the whole Kenney clip set and renders through the existing spectral material path. ASSET_CATALOG §4 records the delta.

### Balance
- **The v0.4 duo re-run, and what it actually found.** Three 60-match `--free-duos` sweeps (n≈120 games per champion) against the first harness that has ever dealt duos. **Rook was the outlier, not the new champions**: he finished top in both early runs (59%, 61%) and turned up in four of the six strongest duo pairings, so his Stonewall block cadence goes **8 s → 9.5 s**. That one change pulled the whole top of the table in — the fresh-seed verification run lands every champion inside the 40–60% rail (Grukk 60 · Rook 53 · Rattle 52 · Boltz 51 · Fathom 48 · Sylva 47 · Mortis 46 · Wisp 44), a 16-point spread against the ±25% of v0.3. Wisp also got a small control buff on the way (Ectoplasm chill 10% → 13%, Haunting Hour 54 → 58/s) — worth keeping on its merits, though it should be said plainly that it did **not** move her winrate; she stays last because a stealth/decoy/fear kit is the hardest thing in the game for a bot to pilot, and buffing her further would be tuning for the harness rather than for players. Numbers mirrored into docs/CHAMPIONS.md.
- **First-pass tuning for the two new champions,** measured over ~150 mixed-tier bot matches. **Boltz** ran hot (64% / 73% across two seeds, best KDA in the game) — an un-missable hitscan beam is worth more to a bot than any skillshot, so it now prices that in: Arc Zapper `70 (+55% AD)` → `60 (+48% AD)`, Capacitor bonus `25 (+30% AD)` → `18 (+25% AD)` and its arc radius 4.5 → 3.5 u. He lands at 60% on the wider 48-match run. **Wisp** ran cold (24% / 36%) — the squishiest frame in the game attached to a kit (stealth, decoy, fear zones) that bots cannot use well: HP 590 → 625, armor 19 → 21, ward 26 → 27, Boo `75` → `84`, Haunting Hour `45/s` → `54/s`. Her personal performance moved clearly (KDA 4.7/7.7 → 6.1/6.8, now mid-pack), though her winrate has not yet followed — that run's team-0 skew (65%) makes a low-n winrate read unreliable, so the honest verdict is deferred to the nightly sweep rather than chased with more small samples. All numbers mirrored into docs/CHAMPIONS.md.

## [Unreleased] — v0.3.0 "Online" (in progress)

### Added
- **`@mini-clash/server` — the authoritative game server (TECH §2/§5):** a Colyseus room hosts the same deterministic sim the offline worker runs, stepped at 30 Hz with 20 Hz downstream snapshots. Everything a client sends is validated server-side: seat identity comes from the connection (wire player ids are ignored), intents are rate-limited (30/s), trainer/debug intents are rejected, and rosters are shape-checked with a safe default fallback. Per-team snapshot views keep brush concealment and ping scoping server-enforced — a modified client cannot see through brush or read enemy pings. Includes an `/healthz` endpoint, seat-hold + bot cover on disconnect (90 s reclaim window), and fake-lag knobs (`MC_FAKE_LAG_MS` / `MC_FAKE_JITTER_MS` / `MC_FAKE_LOSS`) for netcode testing. Boots via an esbuild launcher (`packages/server/run.mjs`); room behavior covered by a real socket round-trip test suite.
- **Online play through one net interface:** the client's game runtime now speaks to a `NetLink` adapter — `WorkerLink` (offline Web Worker sim) and `SocketLink` (Colyseus) are interchangeable, so offline and online share every system above the transport. `?online=1` routes Bridge Brawl through the server (`?server=` overrides the endpoint; production defaults to same-origin `wss://…/ws`). The HUD shows a color-coded RTT chip online, and a transport drop lands on a clean failure veil ("Match lost to the void" + reason + back to hub) — never a stuck client.
- **Movement prediction + reconciliation (TECH §6):** the local champion starts walking the instant you click — a client-side navgrid walk at authoritative move speed — while the server confirms one trip later. Reconciliation compares the server state against the *recent predicted trajectory* (a snapshot is one trip old; riding behind on the walked path is on-track, not an error), drains genuine divergence rate-capped (~10 u/s, ≤0.8 u per snapshot, every step glide-smoothed over 100 ms) so corrections read as a catch-up slide instead of a rubber-band, and hard-snaps only when the server itself jumped (dash/hook/respawn). Remote entities interpolate 100 ms behind arrival through a snapshot ring with a 100 ms extrapolation cap. The rules live in a pure, unit-tested module (`predict.ts`, 8 tests).
- **Lag-simulation acceptance smoke (`scripts/smoke-lag.mjs`, in CI):** boots the real server with 150 ms delay + 30 ms jitter + 2% loss and plays an online match in headless Chromium, asserting the click-to-move response beats the round trip (~0.35 u moved within 250 ms at ~650 ms measured RTT), no correction step ever reads as a jump (≤1 u), and divergence stays bounded — the ROADMAP "playable at 150 ms/2% loss" checkbox, executable.
- **Death-screen damage recap (UI_UX §10):** the death screen now opens with **"What killed you"** — the top 3 damage sources of your last 12 seconds, each with the killer's champion chip, the exact ability (Q/W/R name with its icon), attacks, passives, items, or structure, and a relative damage bar. Attribution runs through the sim's single damage funnel: every ability cast tags its slot, spawned projectiles and kegs carry their casting slot to impact, item procs and passives label themselves, and structure/mini damage classifies by source — so the recap teaches real counterplay, not guesses. Frozen at the moment of death, cleared on respawn, delivered through the codec's rare block (one resend per death). Covered by sim tests: per-ability aggregation, top-3 ordering, the 12 s window expiring old chip damage, and respawn clearing.
- **Quick-chat (GAME_DESIGN §17):** hold **C** for a 4-phrase radial — Nice! / Thanks! / Help! / On it! — mirroring the ping wheel. Lines are team-scoped, validated against a server-side whitelist, rate-limited (700 ms), and never AFK-flag you; they land as gold-edged toast lines under the ally frames with a soft cue. Offline the wheel echoes locally. Server test proves the enemy team never hears you and free-text injection dies at the whitelist.
- **Deploy stack, observability & soak (TECH §1/§11/§14):** `DOMAIN=play.example.com docker compose up -d --build` is now the whole production story — a multi-stage `Dockerfile` produces a **game** image (the Colyseus server as one self-contained esbuild bundle, verified to boot standalone) and a **web** image (Caddy with automatic HTTPS serving the built client and reverse-proxying everything under `/ws` — websockets, matchmake calls, lobby lookups — to the game), plus an uptime-kuma **status** page; CI builds the compose images per commit. The server got real observability: pino JSON logs for every room lifecycle event and Prometheus metrics at `/metrics` (live rooms/clients, sim-tick histogram, snapshot bytes, join/leave churn). The **200-socket soak** runs in CI as its own job: 25 rooms × 8 real WebSocket clients issuing orders for 45 s — every client's sim keeps advancing, tick p95 holds the ≤ 4 ms budget, downstream stays ~3 KB/s per client, and memory stays flat within bounds. README documents the VPS deploy, staging, and the dev server path.
- **Binary delta-snapshot codec (TECH §6):** online snapshots now travel as binary frames instead of JSON — positions quantized to 0.01 u, timers to 0.1 s, and every entity split into dirty-masked blocks (position / vitals / progress+stats / cooldowns / cast-state / buffs / passive readouts / rare-JSON) so only what changed crosses the wire and idle entities cost nothing. Baselines (self-contained keyframes with the full string table) go out every ~2 s and immediately when a client joins or rejoins mid-match, so late arrivals and refreshers key cleanly into the stream. Damage and fx events — the teamfight firehose — encode binary too; low-rate events stay JSON. Ticking passive readouts (Rook's Stonewall timer, Sylva's pollen tracker) get their own compact block precisely because JSON would have resent them every frame. The per-client intent ack is patched into a fixed header offset on a per-client copy of the shared team buffer. Measured on a real elite-bot mid-game brawl: **~10.4 KB/s per client average (409 B typical delta, 5.1 KB keyframes)** against the ≤ 12 KB/s budget — enforced forever by `codec-budget.test.ts`, with round-trip fidelity, brush re-entry, late-join, and periodic-resync covered in the protocol test suite. All online smokes (lag, lobby, reconnect) now run over this wire.
- **Reconnect, AFK takeover & clean failure (GAME_DESIGN §17):** every seat message carries a one-time **rejoin ticket** persisted to sessionStorage — closing or refreshing the tab mid-match boots straight back into the *same* match (clock continuous, same seat, ~6 s on the CI machine; a used ticket mints a fresh one, a dead room burns it so boot never loops). While you're gone the server's cover bot plays your seat and hands it back the moment you return. Going **AFK** (45 s without input) does the same thing live: a bot takes over, an amber banner says so, and any input reclaims the seat instantly. Clean failure got teeth too: `scripts/smoke-reconnect.mjs` in CI kills the server mid-match and asserts the "Match lost to the void" veil inside 10 s with a working route home. Dropping during the loading screen now also bot-covers your seat instead of stranding it.
- **Custom lobbies with codes (UI_UX §5):** "Play with friends" in the hub creates a lobby or joins one by 6-char code (`?join=ABC123` copy-links deep-link straight into the join sheet). The lobby screen shows two team columns of 4 seats — tap an empty seat to move there; the leader drops bots on seats and tunes each one's tier (Recruit/Veteran/Elite) right on the chip — plus a huge invite code with copy-code/copy-link, a bot-fill toggle, non-leader readiness, and a leader START gated on everyone being ready. Edge states are real: crown migration with a toast when the leader leaves, a friendly "make your own lobby" error on dead/full codes, mid-select deserters become bots that keep their pick. START runs the champion-select deal **server-side** (per-human deal + 2 rerolls, shared team bench with atomic swaps, 45 s auto-lock, bot picks dealt with the same no-duplicate rule) and hands every human a one-time seat reservation into a private BridgeRoom — walk-ins without a token are rejected, wire identity stays server-assigned. The lobby survives the match: the hub shows an ambient party dock while you browse, and Play Again returns the whole party to the same lobby for the next round. The select ceremony itself is now presentation-only (`SelectCeremony` + a driver), so offline and lobby matches share the exact same deal UI, and the whole HUD renders ally/enemy relative to *your* seat (team frames, killfeed, minimap — mirrored for team 1 so your base is always left — scoreboard, victory slab, podium, fountain/gate tinting): sitting on the red side reads identically to the blue side. Room loading now waits for every reserved human (20 s cap per UI_UX §7; a bot stands in until the slow loader arrives). Covered by a full server round-trip test suite and a CI smoke that drives two real browsers — create, deep-link join, bots, ready/start, server deal, both land in the same online match.

- **Nightly balance sweep:** a scheduled workflow (`.github/workflows/nightly-balance.yml`) runs 210 mixed-tier bot matches across three seed shards every night (`workflow_dispatch` for on-demand runs with a custom size), each shard emitting a JSON summary (`botmatch.mjs --json`) that `balance-aggregate.mjs` folds into a combined winrate table on the run summary — the big-n read the balance annotations point at, where ±7–8% small-sample noise stops dominating. Red only when matches fail to finish; band status is a report. The game server also logs a **sim state hash every 30 s** per room, arming the ROADMAP "no desync" spot checks for the first real multi-human session.

### Changed
- **Bot micro (veteran and elite):** bots now **focus-fire the softest effective target** in reach (HP × armor weighting — a healthy paper-armor gunner outranks a half-dead vanguard), **commit to closing on longer-ranged harassers** instead of eating free poke on the march, but only with local numbers (never diving a supported kiter — the ungated version measurably fed), and **aim heal-bearing casts at the sorest ally in range** instead of the enemy's feet (data-driven: any ability whose actions heal qualifies — Sylva's Blooming Ward today).
